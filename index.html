On data control

The internet's first act was to move information anywhere. Its second act, the one we're living through now, is different: for the first time, that information is being read, interpreted, and acted on by something other than a human, an agent that doesn't sleep, doesn't ask permission, and doesn't share a person's judgment about what's sensitive. The shift is large enough that even the Vatican noticed it, issuing its first doctrinal note on artificial intelligence in January 2025, Antiqua et Nova, observing that tasks once reserved for people are now routinely handed to machines.

Every earlier layer of the internet needed its own place to enforce trust: firewalls for the network, access control for the database. Agents now move data across steps nobody is standing in front of. I think the next layer has to watch from inside that motion, not around it.
